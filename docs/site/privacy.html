Queer Frame privacy policy

Queer Frame for iPhone and, once it is released, Android. Last updated 2026-10-30.

The short version

Queer Frame collects nothing about you. There is no account, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party code of any kind in the iPhone app (the Android app's open-source libraries are named under "On Android").

What stays on your device

If you turn on new-episode reminders, your iPhone schedules them itself from the app's data file, as local notifications. They are not sent to or through any server, and the app learns nothing from them. You can turn them off at any time, in the app or in Settings.

Favorites are stored on your device only. The app never sends them anywhere and never syncs them. Like other app data, they are included in your own iCloud or computer backup of the device, and they are removed when you delete the app.

You can also save them to a backup file yourself: on the Favorites screen, tap the … menu, then Export favorites. That file lists only which shows and characters you favorited and when. It goes wherever you send it from the share sheet (Files, AirDrop, or anywhere else you choose), and never to the developer. Import favorites reads a backup file you pick, on this device or another.

If you add the Up Next widget, the app writes a small file for it on your device: the titles of your favorite shows and their next episodes' numbers and dates. The widget reads only that file and makes no network request. The file never leaves your device and is removed when you delete the app.

The one network request, and who serves it

When you open the app, when you pull to refresh, and when you return to the app while its data is more than 3 days old (at most once every 6 hours), the app requests one file: the public dataset at chelseakr.github.io/queer-tv-guide/snapshot.v1.json. The request carries no account, no cookie and no identifier created by the app.

That file is served by GitHub, through GitHub Pages. Like any web server, GitHub sees the IP address of every request, together with a standard User-Agent (the app's name and version and your iOS version). GitHub states: "When a GitHub Pages site is visited, the visitor's IP address is logged and stored for security purposes" (GitHub Pages documentation). That log belongs to GitHub and is governed by the GitHub Privacy Statement. The developer of Queer Frame does not receive it and cannot see it.

This website

The pages of this site, including the per-show pages that answer "does she die in …?", are plain files. They set no cookies, run no analytics, ask for no account, contain no form, and load nothing from any other site: no script, font, image, stylesheet or tracker. They carry a small block of search-engine data that describes the page and runs no code. The answer on a show page is hidden until you open it, and opening it sends nothing anywhere. Like the data file above, the pages are served by GitHub through GitHub Pages, which logs the IP address of every request as described there; the developer does not receive that log. Links to LezWatch.TV, TVmaze and other sites send nothing unless you follow them, and then that site's own privacy policy applies.

Age range

Where a state law requires it (Texas, and from 2027 California, Alabama, Utah and Louisiana), the iPhone app asks Apple, when it opens, for the age range of the person using it: under 13, 13 to 15, 16 to 17, or 18 and over. Apple decides whether to share it. The app uses the answer only to meet those laws. It keeps it in memory while the app is open, never saves it, never sends it to the developer or anyone else, and shows and changes nothing because of it. Where no law applies, the app does not ask. Apple handles age checks, parental approval and purchases by minors.

Links you open

Where-to-watch links and links to LezWatch.TV and TVmaze open outside the app, in Safari or in the linked service's app. What those sites collect is governed by their own privacy policies.

The Share button on a show hands that show's LezWatch.TV page address to the iOS share sheet. You choose where it goes; the app adds nothing to the address and sends nothing itself.

On Android

Queer Frame for Android is not released yet. As built, it works the same way and collects nothing. It makes the same one request for the same file, with no account, no cookie and no identifier created by the app. Its User-Agent is set by the app to its name, its version and the word Android (for example "QueerFrame/1.0.0 (Android)"); it does not name your phone's model or your Android version. Reminders are set by your phone itself as local notifications, and nothing goes through any server. Favorites stay on your device: they are left out of Google's cloud backup and move only if you transfer data directly from an old phone to a new one. Export favorites saves the backup file wherever you choose. The app is built with Android's open-source AndroidX libraries and Kotlin's; it contains no Google Play services, analytics, advertising or tracking code. Links open in your browser or the linked app, and Share hands a show's address to Android's share sheet.

Children

Queer Frame collects no data from anyone, including children. The age range the iPhone app may receive from Apple (see Age range) stays in memory on the device and is never collected.
